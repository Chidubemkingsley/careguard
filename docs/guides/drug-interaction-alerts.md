# Drug Interaction Alerts for Caregivers

CareGuard can check the medications on a care recipient's profile for possible interactions. The result is a prompt to review the combination, not a diagnosis or a substitute for advice from a pharmacist or clinician.

## Where to find an alert

After an interaction check has run, open the **Medications** tab. The interaction panel appears below the tracked medication list and shows the check's summary. Any findings are listed with the medication names, a severity label, and a recommendation. Severe, moderate, and lower-severity findings use different warning colors so they are easier to distinguish.

If no interaction panel is visible, a check may not have run for the current result yet. The panel reflects the results returned by the check; it is not a continuous monitor of every medication change.

## What happens if a possible interaction is found

The alert does **not** automatically stop a medication order, and the warning alone does not require your approval. The agent may continue with an order if the requested task and spending policy allow it. The usual payment safeguards still apply: an order at or above your approval threshold is placed in the **Approvals** tab, where its countdown applies; an order outside a firm spending limit is blocked.

If an order is waiting for you, review its details and cancel it before the countdown ends if you do not want it to proceed. A payment that reaches the end of its countdown without being cancelled is approved automatically. See [The Approvals Tab](approvals-tab.md) for details.

## What you should do

Read the recommendation and contact a pharmacist or the care recipient's clinician about what it means for their specific medicines and health. Do not start, stop, or change a medication based only on this alert. If you are concerned about a pending order, cancel it before its countdown ends and ask a pharmacist before proceeding.

Interaction checks can miss issues and may not include every medication, dose, supplement, or personal health factor. **Always consult a pharmacist or clinician for medical advice.**

For more about the medication results panel, see [Medications Tab](medications-tab.md). For the agent's other capabilities and limits, see [What the Agent Can Do](what-the-agent-can-do.md).
